Generated by `python -m eval.evaluate`.

## 1. Triage

Two labeled sets of messages in English, Hindi, Tamil and romanized Hindi/Tamil. Both were written by the author and are **not** real disaster data.

- **Development set** (60 messages, `data/triage_training.csv`): used to tune the lexicon and to train the fallback classifier. Its scores are optimistic by construction.
- **Held-out set** (32 messages, `heldout_messages.csv`): written and labeled *before* being run, never used for tuning. **These are the numbers that matter.**

| Metric | Development | Held-out |
|---|---|---|
| Need category accuracy | 100% | **91%** |
| Urgency level exact match | 33% | **22%** |
| Urgency level within one step | 98% | **84%** |
| Critical messages ranked HIGH or CRITICAL (safety recall) | 100% (22/22) | **71%** (10/14) |
| Critical messages ranked LOW (worst case) | 0 | **0** |

## 2. Dispatch: optimal assignment vs first-come-first-served

Averaged over **200 random flood scenarios** across Chennai (40 requests, 14 volunteers with mixed skills and capacities each). Both strategies respect skills, capacity and a 15 km limit.

| Metric (mean per scenario) | Optimal (Hungarian) | First-come-first-served | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical requests served | 84.3% | 54.5% | +29.8 pts |
| Total urgency served | 1158 | 984 | +17.7% |
| Requests served | 22.6 | 21.7 | +4.2% |
| Travel per assignment (km) | 6.27 | 7.35 | -14.7% |
| Average ETA (min) | 25.1 | 29.4 | -14.6% |

Scenarios are synthetic (random locations and urgencies), so these numbers show the *relative* benefit of optimal assignment, not real-world response times.
